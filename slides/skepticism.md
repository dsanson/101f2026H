## Direct Observation

Using your sense organs to obtain information that is immediately available to you about your surrounding environment.

:::notes
What are some things you know by DO? Some things you know, but not by DO?
:::

## Induction

Induction is when you reason from a regularity in what you have directly observed to a conclusion about things that you have not directly observed.

---

:::{.argument}
1.  Everything is wet (direct observation)
2.  In the past, I've directly observed that this happens after it rains.
3.  So, it was raining. (not directly observed)
:::

:::notes
How do you know (2)?

Not *foolproof*---things could be wet for some other reason. But, as we ordinarily think of things, reasonable.
:::

## Induction {contenteditable=true}

:::{.argument}
1. In the past, the sun has always set in the west (direct observation)
2. So, the sun will set in the west tonight. (not directly observed)
:::

:::notes
Is this a good inference? Can we improve it by appeal to astronomy?
:::

## FLP

Future Like Past (FLP)
:   Future states of the world will be like past states of the world.

If you didn't believe something like FLP, then you wouldn't think that facts about your past observations gave you any evidence for claims about the future.



:::notes
Induction---at least, future-oriented induction---seems to presuppose FLP.
:::

## Against Knowing the Future

::: {#argument-KF .argument}

- (KF1) If you are not justified in believing that FLP is true, then your
  belief that the sun will set in the west tomorrow is unjustified.
- (KF2) You are not justified in believing that FLP is true.
- (KF3) So, your belief that the sun will set in the west tomorrow is
  unjustified.
- (KF4) If your belief that the sun will set in the west tomorrow is
  unjustified, then you don't know that the sun will set in the west tomorrow.
- (KF5) So, you don't know that the sun will set in the west tomorrow.

:::

:::notes
This generalizes to any belief about the future.
**All of your beliefs about how things will be in
the future are unjustified**. You don't know what's going to happen one year
from now, one hour from now, or even one second from now.
:::




## Knowledge and Justification

## KF1 and FLP

::: {#argument-FF .argument}
## The Faulty Foundation Argument {-}

- (FF1) Your belief that the sun will set in the west tomorrow is based on FLP.
- (FF2) If a belief is based on something that you aren't justified in
  believing, then that belief itself is unjustified.
- (KF1) So, if you are not justified in believing that FLP is true, then your
  belief that the sun will set in the west tomorrow is unjustified.

:::

:::notes

if they get hung up on ways that the future won't resemble the past:

:::{#thesis-FLP-star .thesis}
Future Like Past in Expected Ways (FLP\*)
:   Future states of the world will be like past states of the world *except
    in respects in which we can expect them to differ*.
:::

## KF2 and trouble for FLP

::: {#argument-UJ .argument}
## FLP is Unjustified {-}

- (UJ1) If your belief in FLP is justified, then it is either justified by
  direct observation or by inductive reasoning.
- (UJ2) Your belief in FLP isn't justified by direct observation.
- (UJ3) Your belief in FLP isn't justified by inductive reasoning.
- (KF2) So, your belief in FLP is unjustified.

:::

:::notes
The key here is UJ3. 

UJ1: There are other sources of knowledge---introspection, mathematical proof. But these won't get you knowledge of the external world.

UJ2: You cannot directly observe the future.

Refection question:

1.  The argument turns on the claim that induction and direct
    observation are the only ways of knowing about the world. Is that true?
    When a detective solves a crime unlike any crime she's ever seen before,
    is she using resources other than induction and direct observation? If so,
    how might that help with resisting the argument?
:::

## The Anti-Circularity Argument for UJ3

::: {#argument-AC .argument}

- (AC1) All inductive reasoning about the future assumes the truth of FLP.
- (AC2) If all inductive reasoning about the future assumes the truth of FLP,
  then any inductive reasoning about FLP is circular.
- (AC3) No belief can be justified by circular reasoning.
- (UJ3) So, FLP isn't justified by inductive reasoning.

:::

:::notes

AC2: Circularity: when the conclusion also appears as a premise.

AC3: Using the Magic 8-Ball to check whether the Magic 8-Ball can be trusted. Asking ChatGPT if what it just said is actually true.

:::
